## Guarantee (Harvey-reviewed Sep 29)

**Spoken line (say this exact line):**

"If the problem we agree to fix isn't fixed by the date we set, we keep working on it free, and your monthly plan doesn't start until it's fixed. That date assumes we get the logins and answers we need from you. If we're waiting on you, it moves."

On the call: in Step 3, say the spoken line word for word. In Step 4, when you lock the start date, add: "Your part is getting us logins and answering questions within a day or two. If that slips, the date slips with it."

**Written recap version:**

"Guarantee: We agree to fix [exact problem, in your words]. It counts as fixed when it passes these checks and is accepted under Section 4: [simple check you can see, e.g. every new web lead gets a text back within 5 minutes and shows up in your CRM]. Target date: [date]. If it isn't fixed by then, we keep working on it at no charge, and your monthly plan starts only once it's fixed. The date moves one day for every day we're waiting on access, logins, or answers from you, or on an outside vendor, and we'll tell you in writing whenever we're waiting. If you change or add to the problem, we'll agree on a new date together. If it still isn't fixed 30 days after the target date, either of us can end things and you owe nothing. This guarantee covers the one problem above, not new requests."

In the recap after the call, write down the exact problem we agreed to fix, the "counts as fixed when" check, the target date, and the written guarantee above, so the scope can't drift.

**Process notes:**

- End the recap with: "Reply 'confirmed' so we're on the same page." That reply is the only written proof of the deal until there's a real service agreement.
- Log every "we're waiting on you" notice with its date, by email or text.
- The 30-day walk-away is Harvey's suggested number. Abhi can pick 14 or 45 instead.
- Later: once the first few clients sign, turn the recap into a short service agreement (guarantee, liability cap, cancellation terms) with a quick attorney review.

## 0. Open (1 min)

"Hey [Name], thanks for jumping on. This is a free technical checkup. About 20 minutes.
My only job today is to find where time or money is slipping through in the business. No sales pitch at the start. Cool?"

Wait for yes.

"I'll ask five quick questions, we'll name the biggest leak in your words, and if there's a clear fix I'll show you two flat monthly options. Sound good?"

## 1. Ask (8 min)

Ask all five. Dig one layer on anything that hurts. Write their words down. Do not invent numbers.

**Required on every call:** always write down their typical job or order value. If it doesn't come up, ask: "What's a typical job or order worth to you?" We use it in the 30-day value recap.

**Q1. Leads**

"When a new lead comes in, what happens next? Who grabs it, and how fast?"

If slow or unclear:

"Roughly how many new leads a week? And of those, how many actually become a booked job or sale?"

**Q2. Follow-ups**

"After someone doesn't answer or says 'later,' how do you follow up? Is that written down somewhere, or does it live in your head?"

If messy:

"Any sense of how many warm leads went quiet in the last month?"

**Q3. Busywork**

"What work eats the most of your week that isn't selling or delivering? Scheduling, reminders, quotes, paperwork, chasing people?"

If they name something:

"How many hours a week does that eat for you or your team?"

**Q4. Tools that don't talk**

"What tools do you use day to day? Phone, texts, email, CRM, booking, invoices, inventory?"

Then:

"Where do you re-type the same info, or lose something between tools?"

**Q5. After-hours**

"What happens when someone calls or messages after you close, or while you're with a customer?"

If they miss them:

"About how many of those a week? And what's a typical job or order worth when one of those turns into work?"

**Optional pulse (only if they open up):**

"On a scale of 1 to 10, how much is this leaking stuff bugging you right now?"

## 2. Name the leak (4 min)

Pick the one or two biggest problems. Mirror their words. Then build a rough monthly cost with them on the call. Never invent a figure they didn't help with.

"Here's what I'm hearing. The biggest leak is [their words]. Second is [their words]. Did I get that right?"

Wait.

"Let's put a rough dollar number on it so we're not guessing. You said about [X leads / calls / hours] per [week/month], and a typical job is about $[Y]. If even [Z] of those slip each month, that's roughly $[Y times Z] a month. Does that feel low, high, or about right?"

Adjust with them until they nod.

"So we're talking about something like $[agreed]/month tied to [leak in their words]. Fair?"

If they won't ballpark:

"No problem. We can leave the dollar blank and still fix the process. The pain is still [their words]."

---

## 3. Offer the fix (5 min)

Only after they agree on the leak.

"Okay. Two ways we can fix this. Both are a flat monthly price. No per-session nickel-and-diming.
**Get Covered** starts at $199 a month. We cover the leak we just named so it stops bleeding while you keep running the business.
**Full Fix** is the deeper version. We dig further across the ops gaps we touched (leads, follow-ups, busywork, tools, after-hours) and clean them up as one plan.
Either way, the guarantee is simple:
If the problem we agree to fix isn't fixed by the date we set, we keep working on it free, and your monthly plan doesn't start until it's fixed. That date assumes we get the logins and answers we need from you. If we're waiting on you, it moves.
We plug the leak in how work moves: who catches the lead, what happens next, and what stops falling through. If missed calls are the leak, one fix we can put in is a receptionist that answers your calls, books the job, and texts you back."

Pause.

"Which sounds closer to what you need: Get Covered for the one leak, or Full Fix for the wider clean-up?"

If they ask price on Full Fix and you don't have a number locked:

"I'll quote Full Fix as a flat monthly number based on what we scoped today. Want me to send that with the start date?"

Do not change price on the spot to force a yes.

---

## 4. Book the next step (2 min)

Before you hang up, lock a start date.

"Great. Let's pick a start date while we're here so this doesn't sit in limbo. Looking at your week, what day works to kick off: [Day A] or [Day B]?"

Confirm:

"Locked: we start [date]. I'll send a short recap of the leak we named, the plan you picked, that start date, and the written guarantee: the exact problem we're fixing, how we'll both know it's fixed, and the target date."

Then add:

"Your part is getting us logins and answering questions within a day or two. If that slips, the date slips with it."

Then say:

"About 30 days after the fix is live, we'll send you a short recap showing what it caught, in your numbers."

If they say yes but waffle on date:

"Totally fine if the work starts next week. I just need a date on the calendar so the fix has a finish line. Which day?"

## Objection A: "I need to think about it"

Don't argue the stall. Find the real concern, then close on clarity.

"Totally fair. What's your main concern? What are you afraid might happen if we start?"

Listen. Answer that one thing.

Then (pick one that fits):

**Main concern close**

"Got it. So the worry is [their concern]. Here's how we handle that: [one sentence]. Feeling clearer on that?"

**Hypothetical**

"If this were a perfect fit, would you start? ... Okay, what's the gap between perfect and what we just scoped?"

**Best case / worst case (use the guarantee)**

"Two paths, both low risk because of the guarantee. Path one: we start, we fix [leak], life gets quieter. Path two: we start, and if we miss the date we set while we have what we need from you, we keep working on it free and your plan doesn't start until it's fixed. Path three: we don't start, and [leak] keeps costing about $[agreed]/month. Which path do you want?"

**1 to 10**

"On a 1 to 10, where 10 is 'let's start,' where are you? ... What would make it a 10?"

Then book the start date. Don't leave with "I'll think." Leave with a date or a clear no.

## Objection B: "Is this the AI phone thing again?"

(RobotDepot customers will ask this.)

"Straight answer: it can be part of the fix, but it's not where we start.
We start with where money and time leak in the business: leads that sit, follow-ups that die, busywork, tools that don't talk, after-hours misses. Then we fix the one we agree on.
If missed or after-hours calls are the leak, an AI receptionist that answers, books, and texts back is one fix we can put in. If calls aren't the leak, we don't push it.
If a phone tool is already in the mix from RobotDepot or anyone else, we leave that alone unless you ask us to connect something. Today is a checkup on the leaks, not a phone pitch."

If they push:

"ActivateAI diagnoses first. We only build what fixes the leak we named. Cool?"
